In-Depth Overview

Key Kidney Conditions Explained

Understanding common kidney diseases and how specialist medical care protects your quality of life.

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

A gradual, progressive loss of kidney filtering capacity spanning Stages 1 through 5. Often silent in early stages, management focuses on blood pressure control, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone blockade, lifestyle adjustment, and dietary protein monitoring.

Diabetic Nephropathy

Kidney damage caused by chronic elevation of blood glucose, damaging the delicate glomerular filtering capillaries. Characterized by early microalbuminuria and progressive scarring. Timely intervention can significantly delay or prevent dialysis.

Elevated Creatinine & Urea

Serum creatinine is a key metabolic waste product used to calculate estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR). An elevated creatinine value signals reduced renal filtration and requires urgent investigation to distinguish reversible from chronic causes.

Proteinuria & Foamy Urine

Healthy kidneys keep albumin and essential blood proteins within the bloodstream. Persistent foamy urine or positive protein in urinalysis indicates a breach in glomerular filtration barriers and requires specialized quantification and etiology review.

Kidney Stones & Metabolic Prevention

While acute stone obstruction may require urological intervention, medical nephrology focuses on metabolic workup (24-hour urine chemistry, hypercalciuria, hyperoxaluria, hyperuricemia) and medical dietary strategies to prevent painful recurrent stone formation.

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

A sudden decline in kidney function often precipitated by dehydration, severe infection (sepsis), surgical stress, heart failure decompensation, or toxic exposure to medications like NSAID painkillers or certain antibiotics.

Clinic Checklist

When to Schedule a Nephrology Visit

Consult Dr. Jithin Lal if you experience any of the following signs or laboratory findings:

  • Routine blood tests reveal serum creatinine above reference range or dropping eGFR
  • Urinalysis detects protein, microalbumin, red blood cells, or persistent cellular casts
  • Persistent or unexplained puffiness in the face, eyelids, or swelling in feet and ankles
  • Blood pressure remains persistently elevated despite taking multiple anti-hypertensive drugs
  • You have diabetes and have not had a urine albumin or kidney test in the past 12 months
  • Recurrent kidney stones or a family history of cystic kidney disease or dialysis
