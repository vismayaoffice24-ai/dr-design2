Frequently Asked Questions

When should someone consult a nephrologist?

You should see a nephrologist if routine blood work reveals elevated serum creatinine or urea, if a urinalysis shows protein (albuminuria) or blood (hematuria), if you experience unexplained persistent swelling in your feet or face, if you have difficult-to-control high blood pressure, or if you have diabetes and require regular renal surveillance.

Do I need to fast before coming for my appointment?

Routine nephrology consultations do not require fasting unless you have been specifically requested to undergo a fasting blood sugar or lipid profile on the same morning. You may eat normally and take your regular morning blood pressure medications. Please carry all previous blood, urine, and ultrasound test reports with you.

Can chronic kidney disease (CKD) be cured or reversed?

While established structural scarring in chronic kidney disease cannot be reversed, early detection and appropriate medical management can significantly slow or halt further disease progression. In cases of Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) caused by dehydration, infection, or medication toxicity, kidney function can frequently recover completely once the primary cause is addressed.

Is dialysis always permanent?

No, dialysis is not always permanent. When dialysis is initiated for Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), it often serves as a temporary support mechanism while the kidneys heal. For patients with irreversible Stage 5 Chronic Kidney Disease (End-Stage Renal Disease), dialysis is typically ongoing unless they undergo a successful kidney transplant. Dr. Jithin Lal conducts a thorough evaluation to discuss the prognosis specific to your condition.
